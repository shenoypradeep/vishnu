विष्णुसहस्रनाम-साधना

Viṣṇu Sahasranāma Sādhana

A daily japa sādhana that joins breath, sound and stillness into one sitting

विष्णवे प्रभविष्णवेViṣṇave prabhaviṣṇave · to Viṣṇu, the all-pervading

VSS Sādhana is a complete japa sādhana (practice of sacred repetition) built around the thousand names of Viṣṇu. Prāṇāyāma steadies the breath, the nāmas are chanted with care for their sound and their meaning, and the sitting closes in dhyāna, resting in the quiet the chanting leaves behind. It is practised daily, and together as a sangha in the live Yajña.

शास्त्रप्रमाणम्

Śāstrapramāṇam · Rooted in śāstra

Four passages from three texts hold the practice together. Each is in the Granthālaya, with its word-by-word meaning.

Why · Bhagavad Gītā 10.25

Read in the Granthālaya →

महर्षीणां भृगुरहं गिरामस्म्येकमक्षरम् । यज्ञानां जपयज्ञोऽस्मि स्थावराणां हिमालयः ॥ २५ ॥

Of the great seers I am Bhṛgu; of utterances I am the one syllable; of sacrifices I am the sacrifice of silent repetition; of immovable things, the Himālaya.

Yajñānāṃ japa-yajño 'smi: among yajñas, Bhagavān names japa as himself. This is why VSS is a japa yajña, and why its group sessions are called Yajña.

The method · Yoga Sūtra 1.27–28

Read in the Granthālaya →

तस्य वाचकः प्रणवः ॥ २७ ॥ तज्जपस्तदर्थभावनम् ॥ २८ ॥

The word that denotes him is the syllable Om. The repetition of it and dwelling on its meaning (are to be practised).

Tad-artha-bhāvanam: japa means dwelling on what is chanted. The sūtras speak of the praṇava; VSS applies the same principle to the thousand nāmas, each of which names Īśvara. So the chanting is learned together with the meaning of each nāma.

What it does · Yoga Sūtra 2.1–2

Read in the Granthālaya →

तपःस्वाध्यायेश्वरप्रणिधानानि क्रियायोगः ॥ १ ॥ समाधिभावनार्थः क्लेशतनूकरणार्थश्च ॥ २ ॥

Austerity, recitation and devotion to the Lord are the yoga of action. It is for bringing about meditative absorption and for thinning the afflictions.

Svādhyāya, in Vyāsa's bhāṣya, is the repetition of Om and other purifying formulas, or the study of the scriptures of liberation. VSS does both, chanting the nāmas and studying their meaning; practised with discipline and in surrender, it is kriyā yoga.

How · Bhaja Govindam 30

Read in the Granthālaya →

प्राणायामं प्रत्याहारं नित्यानित्यविवेकविचारम् । जाप्यसमेतसमाधिविधानं कुर्ववधानं महदवधानम् ॥ ३० ॥

Regulation of the breath, withdrawal of the senses, inquiry that discerns the eternal from the transient, and the practice of meditative absorption together with repetition of the mantra: do these with attention, with great attention.

The procedure itself: prāṇāyāma, then japa, then stillness, done with care.

  1. WhyJapa is the highest yajña. Gītā 10.25
  2. The methodChant the name and dwell on its meaning. Yoga Sūtra 1.28
  3. What it doesIt cultivates samādhi and thins the kleśas. Yoga Sūtra 2.1–2
  4. HowPrāṇāyāma, then japa, then stillness. Bhaja Govindam 30

Sādhanākramaḥ · One sitting, three movements

  1. १

    Prāṇāyāma प्राणायामः

    Ujjayi breathing calms and steadies the breath, and the mind with it.

  2. २

    Sahasranāma japa नामजपः

    The thousand names are chanted with correct pronunciation, supported by mudrā, and with an understanding of what each nāma means.

  3. ३

    Dhyāna ध्यानम्

    After the chanting, a time to rest in the quiet it leaves behind.

द्विविधम्

Dvividham · Two dimensions of the practice

The material dimension

The science of chanting

The cadence of the chanting makes each exhalation longer than the inhalation and draws on breathing from the diaphragm. Slow breathing of this kind is linked with vagal activity and a settled nervous system. Vedic Space reads this as the nara, the body, in which Nārāyaṇa dwells.

Two studies are a starting point. Reciting mantras slowed breathing to about six breaths a minute and brought heart-rate, blood-pressure and breathing rhythms into step. A pilot brain-imaging study of OM chanting found limbic deactivation like that reported with vagus nerve stimulation. Both are small studies: they suggest, they do not prove.

  1. Bernardi L. et al. (2001). Effect of rosary prayer and yoga mantras on autonomic cardiovascular rhythms. BMJ 323: 1446–9. doi:10.1136/bmj.323.7327.1446
  2. Kalyani B. G., Venkatasubramanian G., Arasappa R., … Kalmady S. V. et al. (2011). Neurohemodynamic correlates of ‘OM’ chanting: a pilot functional magnetic resonance imaging study. International Journal of Yoga 4(1): 3–6. doi:10.4103/0973-6131.78171

The spiritual dimension

Prāṇa, mantra, mudrā, dhyāna

In the Sahasranāma japa tradition the four come together in one sitting: the breath gathers prāṇa, the nāma is the mantra, the hands hold the mudrā, and the mind settles into dhyāna.

As the sādhana deepens, chanting and meaning stop being separate. As the founders' study circle found, “the nāmas started coming alive.”

What the practice is meant to cultivate

  • शान्तिःInner calm

    A relaxed body, a quiet mind (citta-prasāda), emotional steadiness.

  • वाक्सौम्यम्Gentle speech

    Clarity and warmth in words and relationships.

  • क्षमा करुणाPatience, compassion

    A heart that responds rather than reacts.

  • सहनशक्तिःResilience

    Meeting challenges with ease, even as līlā.

  • स्मृतिः विवेकःClarity

    Steadier attention and discernment.

  • प्रतिभाInsight

    Fresh perspective and intuitive understanding.

  • विश्रान्तिःDeep rest

    A settled nervous system and true rest.

  • उत्साहः ओजःEnthusiasm

    Vitality and inner drive.

  • समत्वम्Evenness

    A felt sense of unity with life and with the Self.

  • सत्त्वम्Sattva

    Inner purity, harmony and brightness.

  • धैर्यम् स्थिरताSteadiness

    Courage, strength and rootedness.

These are what the sādhana aims at, not promised results. Each person's experience is their own.
